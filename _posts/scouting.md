I've been a comittee member at our local Scout troop for over four years now. My son decided to join after being invited by a friend. It was the height of Covid and we were all mostly trapped in our houses. Because Scouts was holding their meetings outside, we could be in-person. I remember being surprised on that day when he told me he wanted to join after we got back in the car after that first meeting. He was never a Cub Scout and our outdoor adventures to date had been in a tent in the backyard. 

My impression of Boy Scouts at the time was only of an organization embroiled in scandal- a failed system with good intentions from another time. What I did not realize then is that the experience that you and your child have in Scouting is almost completely dependent on the local troop leadership. We certainly follow the national program but the troop is run by volunteers and a committee who set the troop on a path to serve its community. I may not agree with everything the national or regional (known as the council) organizations put forth, but we are given the latitude locally to guide the troop for the town(s) we serve. It's an important example of how we may be able to slowly heal this divide our society suffers from so greatly by serving our local communities. Maybe it's Scouting, or a local charity, or your local religious organization. By engaging in your community you are strengthening it. You are also strengthening your ties to other people, creating real relationships that can help you feel less isolated and less helpless. 

I often think back to that day my son decided to join and the reasons he chose to join are the reasons I would urge you to serve your community. Get out of your house and away from your screen. Be with people to help your community and yourself.
